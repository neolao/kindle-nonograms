# Filled cells and color swatches drop their shape glyph — plain solid-color squares only

**Context:** Decision 005 had a filled grid cell render its palette color as a solid `background-color` plus a shape glyph (`● ▲ ■ ◆`, cycling by palette index) drawn on top, specifically so two palette colors of similar luminance would stay distinguishable on Kindle's grayscale e-ink screens. The color-swatch buttons mirrored the same rendering. A bug report ("I still see a white circle in a filled black cell") turned out not to be a defect: the glyph was rendering exactly as decision 005 specified. Clarifying with the Product Owner confirmed the actual request: a filled cell should look like a classic nonogram's plain solid square, with no symbol on top, for every puzzle regardless of how many colors it uses.
**Decision:** `paintCell()`'s filled branch now only sets `background-color`; it draws no glyph and sets no text color. Color-swatch buttons are likewise plain solid-color squares; the only remaining non-color cue is a `✓` checkmark on whichever swatch is currently active (colored via `contrastingTextColor` so it stays legible against any palette color), plus the pre-existing thicker border on the active swatch. Crossed (`marked`) cells are unaffected — they keep their fixed `✖` glyph and no background fill.
**Reason:** Explicit Product Owner decision, reached after two rounds of clarification, overriding the grayscale-disambiguation rationale in decision 005 for the *fill* rendering specifically. A UI/UX and a frontend-design consultation both confirmed the plain-square look is the right simplification and flagged the resulting trade-off (see Rejected alternatives) as worth recording rather than silently dropping.
**Rejected alternatives:** Keeping the glyph only for multi-color puzzles (dropping it just for single-color ones) — rejected by the Product Owner in favor of one consistent rendering everywhere. Reusing the clue-runs' border-style cycle (decision 003) on grid cells or swatches as a color-blind/grayscale-safe replacement for the glyph — not requested; noted here as a possible future mitigation if the accepted trade-off below turns out to matter in practice.

**Accepted trade-off:** On a grayscale e-ink screen, two palette colors of similar luminance can now render as visually identical filled squares (or swatch buttons) with no glyph to tell them apart — this was the exact failure mode decision 005 was written to prevent. For puzzles built from a single black palette color (`packages/shared/src/adapters.ts`'s `fromBooleanGridExport`, the common case for content converted from the sibling generator project), this has no practical effect: a solid block of the one color, adjoined into a run, is the intended classic look. The risk applies to hand-authored multi-color puzzles whose palette entries are close in luminance; it is accepted as-is, not mitigated, per the Product Owner's explicit call.
