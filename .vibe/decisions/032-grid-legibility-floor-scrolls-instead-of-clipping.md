# The grid-fit legibility floor is a real pixel size, and a puzzle that still doesn't fit at that size scrolls instead of shrinking further or clipping

**Context:** The puzzle page and the editor canvas scale their whole grid (cells + clues) to fit the viewport via a single `font-size`, clamped to a `MIN_GRID_SCALE`. That floor was originally 0.5 (8px) to protect legibility, but a real wide-puzzle clipping bug forced it down to 0.3 (~4.8px) so the grid would keep shrinking until it genuinely fit inside `.grid-wrapper`'s `overflow:hidden` fail-safe — trading legibility away to stop clipping. Backlog item 054 asks for legibility back without reintroducing that clipping.

**Decision:** `MIN_GRID_SCALE` is now derived from a concrete minimum legible font size (10px out of the 16px base, ~0.625) instead of an arbitrary ratio, on both the play page and the editor. `.grid-wrapper`'s CSS switches from `overflow:hidden` to `overflow:auto`. The wrapper's `max-width`/`max-height` cap (still measured off the raw viewport) is kept exactly as before — it now bounds a scrollable box instead of a clipped one. A puzzle whose natural fit would have needed a smaller scale now renders at the legible floor and, if that no longer fits the viewport, is reached by scrolling instead of losing content or shrinking further.

**Reason:** The two previous floors (0.5 and 0.3) each protected exactly one of "legible text" or "nothing invisibly missing" at the other's expense, because both relied on the grid always being small enough to fit with no scrollbar. Making `overflow:auto` the fallback removes that constraint: the legible floor and the no-content-loss guarantee can both hold unconditionally, at the cost of the "no scrollbar, ever" property the original grid-fit design (backlog item 019) deliberately chose. That trade is accepted here as the lesser cost — a large puzzle needing a scroll on a narrow screen is a normal, discoverable interaction; unreadable clue numbers or a silently missing column are not recoverable by the player at all.

**Rejected alternatives:** Keeping the floor at 0.3 and accepting sub-legible text on the largest bundled puzzles — rejected, it is the exact defect item 054 reports. Raising the floor without changing `overflow:hidden` — rejected, it reintroduces the clipping bug the 0.3 floor was created to fix (confirmed by re-deriving the same wide-puzzle scenario from that fix's regression test). Pinning row/column clues in view while scrolling (a frozen header) — considered and left out of this item: `position: sticky` support inside a `<table>` on old WebKit is unreliable, the acceptance criteria doesn't require it, and it is a large enough interaction change to warrant its own item if wanted later.
